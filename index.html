Background & Bio

Deep Technical Expertise in Mechanics & Computation

A career focused on rigorous physical modeling, high-consequence system validation, and pioneering the intersection of scientific computing and machine learning.

I am a research engineer and computational scientist with deep roots in mechanical engineering, mechanics of materials, and computational modeling. I earned my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Carnegie Mellon University, working under Professor Maarten de Boer in the De Boer Group: Mechanics of Materials.

My doctoral research investigated the intricate contact physics, friction phenomena, and surface mechanics of microelectromechanical systems (MEMS), characterizing dynamic friction transitions, surface roughness interactions, and environmental effects at micro and nano scales.

Following my graduate work at CMU, I joined Bettis Atomic Power Laboratory (Naval Nuclear Laboratory), tackling mission-critical engineering problems across structural dynamics, extreme loading shock analysis, non-linear continuum mechanics, and high-performance simulation. I have also served as a Rickover Fellowship Technical Mentor, guiding advanced graduate fellows in advanced computational and experimental engineering.

"By integrating rigorous continuum mechanics principles with modern physics-informed machine learning, we can achieve high-fidelity predictions in a fraction of traditional computational times."

Today, I provide specialized technical consulting, dynamic modeling, and research advisory services to organizations seeking to modernize their scientific modeling pipelines, accelerate numerical simulations, and validate complex mechanical systems.
